🧠 What is Executive N-Back?

The Executive N-Back is a cognitive training task that challenges your working memory and executive function. Unlike the classic N-Back task (where you match the current item to one shown N steps ago), the Executive version adds a transformation step: you must mentally advance the N-back item by one position in a sequence.

Classic N-Back: Press when current = item N-back
Executive N-Back: Press when current = next after item N-back

📋 How It Works

You see a sequence of stimuli (days, months, letters, or shapes). For each stimulus, you must determine:

  • What was shown N positions ago?
  • What comes immediately after that item in the sequence?
  • Does the current stimulus match that "next" item?

If yes — press your response key. If no — withhold your response.

📝 Example (2-Back, Days)

MON → FRI → TUE = PRESS ✓

Two positions back was Monday. The day immediately after Monday is Tuesday. The current stimulus is Tuesday — so you press.

SUN → WED → MON = PRESS ✓

Two positions back was Sunday. The day after Sunday is Monday. Current = Monday — press.

MON → FRI → MON = DO NOTHING ✗

Two positions back was Monday. The day after Monday is Tuesday, not Monday. Even though the item matches the N-back item, this is not a target in Executive N-Back.

🔬 The Study Protocol

The original Executive N-Back study used an adaptive difficulty protocol:

  • 19–20 correct in a block → N increases by 1
  • 16–18 correct → N stays the same
  • 0–15 correct → N decreases by 1 (minimum 1)

Each block contains N warm-up stimuli (not scored) followed by 20 scored decisions with exactly 6 targets and 14 non-targets. Correctly withholding a response on a non-target counts as correct.

Sessions consisted of 20 blocks. The first session began at 1-back, and subsequent sessions started at the previous session's final N minus 2.

📊 What the Research Concludes

Key Finding: The Executive N-Back's transformation requirement ("What comes next?") places significantly higher demands on executive function than classic N-Back. This extra manipulation engages prefrontal cortex activity more intensely, making it a potentially more effective tool for cognitive training.

Research on N-Back training suggests:

  • Regular practice can improve working memory capacity
  • The adaptive protocol ensures users are always working at the edge of their ability
  • Executive N-Back may offer greater transfer effects to real-world cognitive tasks due to its added manipulation component
  • Consistent training over multiple sessions shows the most promise for measurable improvements
Note: While N-Back training shows promise in research, results on far transfer (improvements in untrained cognitive tasks) remain mixed. This app is designed as a cognitive exercise tool, not a clinical intervention.
